Henry G. Peterson

About

I am a postdoctoral associate working with Raffaele Ferrari in MIT's Earth, Atmospheric, and Planetary Sciences Department. I received my Ph.D. in Environmental Science and Engineering from Caltech in 2025, followed by a brief postdoc, both with Jörn Callies. My undergraduate degree was in applied mathematics at UC Berkeley.

In my research, I use a combination of theoretical and numerical models to describe how small-scale ocean dynamics—for varying definitions of small—shape the global climate system. For instance, centimeter-scale turbulence is thought to be crucial for generating the upwelling necessary for closing the meridional overturning circulation. My work shows that there is a two-way coupling between the local response to bottom-enhanced mixing and the basin-scale flow. As part of this work, I developed an open-source finite element model of the large-scale ocean circulation written in Julia. Check out the project highlight on the Schmidt Academy for Software Engineering webpage!

On the other hand, lateral transport of heat and tracers in the ocean is heavily influenced by the dynamics of O(10–100 kilometer)-wide eddies. To better understand how these small-scale features shape the climate system, I am working to couple high-resolution global Oceananigans.jl simulations to a realistic atmosphere model as part of the CliMA project.
